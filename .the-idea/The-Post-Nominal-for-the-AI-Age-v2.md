# The Post-Nominal for the AI Age

## Why `+AI` is not merely an AI disclosure label, but a situational human responsibility declaration

**By Eric Mourant +AI**

---

For centuries, the name attached to a piece of work has carried a quiet but powerful promise.

A signature beneath a letter, a byline above an article, an author’s name on a report, or a developer’s identity attached to a software commit has meant more than *this person was involved*. It has meant:

# **This person stands behind what appears here.**

Until recently, the connection between a person’s name and the work bearing it was comparatively straightforward. A human wrote the letter. A human drew the design. A human prepared the report. A human composed the argument. Tools assisted, but the boundary between the tool and the author remained visible.

Artificial intelligence changes that boundary.

A document may begin as a human idea, become an AI-generated draft, return to the human for criticism, pass back to the machine for restructuring, and undergo several more cycles before anyone sees it. Code may emerge from an extended conversation between a developer and an AI system. An image may combine human direction, machine generation, human selection and machine refinement. A strategic recommendation may contain human judgement informed by analysis that no individual person could have completed alone.

The finished work is no longer easily described as either **human-made** or **AI-made**.

It is something created between them.

Yet someone still sends it.

Someone still publishes it.

Someone still deploys the code, signs the report, submits the research, presents the recommendation or puts their name beneath the result.

That person cannot responsibly say:

> The machine made it, so the outcome is not mine.

This is the problem that `+AI` is designed to solve.

# `+AI`

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

Three characters placed after a name:

# **Eric Mourant +AI**

At first glance, it looks like a small addition to an ordinary signature.

Its strongest contribution, however, is not the plus sign.

It is the transformation in what the mark means and where the mark sits:

**AI disclosure → accountable human declaration**

**disclosure label → situational responsibility post-nominal**

That is the idea at the centre of `+AI`.

---

## The idea is not the punctuation

The notation itself is not the claim to originality.

Placing `+ AI` after an author’s name has been proposed before as a way to disclose that AI was used. The principle that humans remain accountable for AI-assisted work is also established in publishing policies, provenance systems and responsibility protocols.

Those precedents matter.

They prevent an inflated claim that the typography, disclosure or principle of human accountability was invented here. But they also reveal the narrower and more consequential territory of this proposal.

`+AI` is being defined as a single semantic object:

> A mark appended directly to the name of a person or organisation, for a particular work, declaring both that AI materially assisted and that the named party has adopted and accepts responsibility for releasing the result.

The proposal does not merely place two familiar symbols after a name.

It gives that placement a specific function.

The name identifies the responsible party.

The mark acknowledges material AI assistance.

Their combination constitutes the declaration.

As documented in the accompanying [novelty analysis](Novelty-Analysis.md), no earlier proposal has been identified that defines `+AI` specifically in this way: as a **situational responsibility post-nominal**, rather than a credential, generic disclosure sentence, badge or metadata field.

That is a deliberately narrow claim.

It is also the claim worth making.

---

## The semantic transformation

Most approaches to AI transparency begin with disclosure:

* Was AI used?
* Which model was used?
* How much of the work did it generate?
* Which sentences, images or lines of code came from the machine?

These questions describe the production process.

They can be important. In some settings, they are required. But a disclosure can remain impersonal:

**AI-assisted**

**Created with AI**

**Generated in part using artificial intelligence**

Each statement tells us something about the artifact.

None necessarily tells us who has adopted it.

None necessarily tells us who decided it was ready to enter the world.

None necessarily tells us who accepts responsibility for that decision.

`+AI` changes the subject of the statement.

It does not stop at:

> AI was involved.

It continues:

> I knew AI was involved. I chose to use the result. I adopted the work in the form presented. I accept responsibility for releasing it.

This is the semantic transformation:

# **AI disclosure → accountable human declaration**

A disclosure records a fact about a process.

A declaration records a commitment by an identifiable party.

That distinction is the intellectual centre of `+AI`.

---

## The structural transformation

Meaning is only half of the proposal.

Structure matters too.

A disclosure label may sit in a footer, policy statement, metadata record or note. It can describe AI participation without placing responsibility beside a person’s identity.

`+AI` takes a different form:

# **Eric Mourant +AI**

The declaration is attached directly to the responsible party’s name.

That produces the structural transformation:

# **disclosure label → situational responsibility post-nominal**

The post-nominal form does important work.

It compresses identity, AI participation, adoption and responsibility into one human-readable expression:

**identity + declaration**

It remains legible in plain text.

It survives copying, quoting, printing and informal use.

It places the accountable party where responsibility can be seen.

And because it appears only in connection with a particular work, it is situational.

`+AI` does not say:

> Eric Mourant is permanently associated with artificial intelligence.

It says:

> AI materially assisted this work, and Eric Mourant accepts responsibility for releasing this work.

The same person may sign another artifact simply:

**Eric Mourant**

The distinction concerns the associated work, not the permanent character or status of the individual.

## A new kind of post-nominal

Traditional post-nominals usually look backwards.

They tell us what a person has studied, achieved, earned, joined or been authorised to practise. They may identify an academic degree, professional status, public honour or institutional affiliation.

They answer questions such as:

* What has this person accomplished?
* What profession do they belong to?
* Which institution has recognised them?
* What formal authority do they hold?

`+AI` answers a different question:

# **What is this person prepared to take responsibility for now?**

It does not describe a permanent qualification.

It does not certify that someone is an AI expert.

It does not grant status, rank or professional authority.

It describes the relationship between a named party and a particular piece of work.

That makes it a new kind of post-nominal: not a credential attached permanently to a person, but a responsibility declaration attached situationally to an act.

Its force comes from the familiar structure of letters after a name.

Its precision comes from refusing to make those letters permanent.

Each appearance is a fresh declaration.

Each use says:

> For this work, in this form, I acknowledge the assistance and accept the responsibility.

---

## What the declaration contains

When a person or organisation uses `+AI`, the short statement is:

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

The fuller declaration contains several connected ideas.

**AI participation**

Artificial intelligence materially assisted in creating, analysing, transforming, generating, evaluating, structuring or presenting the associated work.

**Knowledge**

The named party knows or reasonably believes that material AI assistance occurred.

**Authority**

The named party authorised, initiated, directed or knowingly adopted the relevant use of AI.

**Control**

The named party retained authority over whether the work would be published, transmitted, deployed, submitted, presented or otherwise released.

**Adoption**

The named party accepts the work in the form in which it is being presented.

**Responsibility**

The named party accepts responsibility for the decision to release, use or represent the work.

These are not six unrelated claims.

Together, they turn an AI-use label into an accountable human declaration.

## Why responsibility matters more than percentage authorship

Imagine a report written through fifty exchanges between a person and an AI system.

The human defines the problem. AI proposes a structure. The human rejects part of it. AI performs analysis. The human notices a flawed assumption. AI recalculates. The human supplies context unavailable to the machine. AI produces a draft. The human changes the conclusion. AI improves the language. The human approves the final document.

Who wrote it?

Any percentage would be arbitrary.

Was it 60 per cent human and 40 per cent AI? Should authorship be measured by word count, originality, intellectual importance, time spent or final editorial control?

The attempt to divide the artifact into neat human and machine portions may tell us less than we imagine.

The more consequential question is:

# **Who decided that this result was ready to enter the world?**

Who chose to send it?

Who accepted the recommendation?

Who authorised the publication?

Who deployed the code?

Who put their name on the outcome?

That is the question `+AI` answers.

The mark does not attempt to solve the metaphysics of authorship.

It establishes a point of accountability.

---

## Augmentation without abdication

The deepest principle behind `+AI` can be expressed in three words:

# **Augmentation without abdication.**

Artificial intelligence can expand what a person is capable of producing. It can accelerate research, reveal patterns, challenge assumptions, generate alternatives and help transform an incomplete idea into a sophisticated result.

But greater capability must not become an excuse for weaker responsibility.

A person cannot reasonably claim the benefit of AI when the result is impressive and disown the machine when the result is defective.

You used the machine.

You selected or adopted the result.

You released it under your name.

You remain responsible for that decision.

This does not mean that a `+AI` declaration guarantees perfection.

It does not promise that every sentence is correct, every calculation has been independently verified or every risk has been eliminated. Responsibility is not the same as infallibility.

Nor does `+AI` replace disclosures required by law, regulation, publishers, employers or professional bodies. A compact responsibility declaration may complement more detailed provenance or compliance information; it should not erase it.

`+AI` is an accountability declaration, not a warranty of correctness and not a substitute for a required disclosure.

Responsibility means that the named party accepts the consequences of the decision to use, adopt and release the work.

The person cannot hide behind the phrase:

> The AI said it.

The machine may have generated the answer.

The human chose to act on it.

`+AI` keeps that human choice visible.

## A model of authorship for collaborative intelligence

Artificial intelligence is forcing us to reconsider what authorship means.

The traditional image of the author is an isolated originator: a person producing words, images or ideas from within the boundaries of an individual mind.

But much human creation has always been collaborative.

Writers work with editors. Architects work with engineers. Researchers build on previous research. Directors shape the performances of actors and the work of cinematographers, designers and composers. Executives sign reports assembled by teams. Software incorporates libraries written by people the developer has never met.

Authorship has never meant that one person performed every underlying act.

It has often meant that one person exercised sufficient direction, judgement, selection and responsibility to place their name on the result.

AI extends that collaborative reality into a new domain.

The machine does not need to be treated as a human co-author for its contribution to be acknowledged. Nor does acknowledging its contribution require the human to surrender responsibility for the work.

`+AI` provides a language for this middle ground.

It says:

> I did not necessarily generate every component unaided.
>
> I did direct, evaluate, select or adopt the final result.
>
> I am the identifiable party who takes responsibility for putting it into the world.

This is not weaker authorship.

In many contexts, it may be a more honest description of authorship than the conventions we use today.

## Situational by design

For `+AI` to retain meaning, it must not become a fashionable decoration permanently attached to a person’s identity.

It is not a badge meaning:

> I use AI.

It is an active declaration meaning:

> AI materially assisted this work, I adopted the result, and I take responsibility for releasing it.

That commitment must be renewed with each use.

### In correspondence

Kindest regards,

**Eric Mourant +AI**

### In a report

**Prepared and accepted by Eric Mourant +AI**

### In software

**Author: Eric Mourant +AI**

### In an article

**By Eric Mourant +AI**

### In visual work

**Created by Eric Mourant +AI**

### In organisational communication

**JAPER Technology +AI**

In every case, the mark identifies the accountable party while acknowledging material AI participation.

Its placement is not ornamental.

The name is what gives the mark its responsibility-bearing force.

## From mark to social convention

Symbols become powerful when people understand them without needing to stop and decode them.

The first time someone sees:

**Eric Mourant +AI**

they may ask what it means.

The answer is simple:

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

The second time, the mark is familiar.

By the tenth time, it begins to operate as a convention.

It does not require registration, certification, payment or membership.

It does not depend on a particular AI provider.

It does not need a proprietary image file.

It can be typed on any keyboard.

Anyone willing to make the declaration should be able to use it.

No permission required.

No gatekeeper.

No approved list of models.

The mark’s credibility comes from the seriousness of the statement made by the party using it.

Without a responsible name, `+AI` is only punctuation.

Beside a name, it becomes a promise.

## The human-facing layer of accountability infrastructure

The visible post-nominal is not a replacement for technical provenance systems.

It is their possible human-facing layer.

Behind the visible `+AI` mark, systems could carry structured information such as:

* the identity of the responsible party;
* the date the declaration was made;
* the class of AI assistance involved;
* the system or model used;
* whether the complete artifact was reviewed;
* whether factual claims were independently verified;
* whether the declaration was cryptographically signed.

A document could display:

**Eric Mourant +AI**

while its metadata records the richer history of how the artifact was created and accepted.

An email could include the mark visibly and carry a corresponding machine-readable header.

A software commit could associate AI assistance with the developer who approved the change.

An image could combine visible attribution with embedded provenance.

Technical systems can preserve detail.

The post-nominal preserves the essential human statement.

The progression is not from no infrastructure to infrastructure. Provenance frameworks and responsibility protocols already exist.

The opportunity is to connect them to a simple public convention:

**human choice → visible declaration → machine-readable record → accountability infrastructure**

The simplicity of `+AI` is therefore not a limitation.

It is the human-readable surface of a potentially much deeper system.

## The agentic future

The need for such a declaration becomes more urgent as artificial intelligence moves beyond generating content and begins taking actions.

AI agents will not merely draft messages. They will send them.

They will not merely recommend purchases. They will transact.

They will not merely suggest code. They will deploy systems.

They will research, negotiate, schedule, monitor, respond and make decisions within delegated boundaries.

In this environment, society will need more than a label saying that AI was present.

We will need to know:

* Under whose authority did the AI act?
* Who established its boundaries?
* Who accepted the outcome?
* Who can be held accountable?
* Where does machine agency end and human responsibility begin?

`+AI` offers a foundational answer:

> The machine assisted or acted, but a named human or organisation remains responsible for the result.

Capability is becoming distributed.

Responsibility must not become diffuse.

---

## Not an AI mark—a human mark

The most important insight about `+AI` is that it is not fundamentally an artificial-intelligence mark.

It is a **situational human responsibility post-nominal for the age of artificial intelligence**.

Its purpose is not to celebrate the machine.

Its purpose is not to shame people for using one.

Its purpose is not to divide the world into pure human work and contaminated AI work.

Its purpose is to preserve the visible connection between power and responsibility.

Artificial intelligence gives individuals access to capabilities once available only to large institutions and specialised teams. That expansion can be liberating, creative and economically transformative.

But every expansion of power creates a corresponding need for accountability.

The proper response is not to deny the technology.

It is to use it openly and own what follows.

# **Use AI openly.**

# **Keep responsibility human.**

## The post-nominal for the AI age

The post-nominals of the past told us what a person had earned.

`+AI` tells us what a person or organisation is prepared to own.

Its intellectual contribution does not depend on claiming the plus sign, AI disclosure or human accountability as new.

It lies in joining them through two deliberate transformations:

# **AI disclosure → accountable human declaration**

# **disclosure label → situational responsibility post-nominal**

The mark is small enough to add to a name:

# **Eric Mourant +AI**

But the statement behind it is substantial:

> Artificial intelligence materially assisted this work.
>
> I knew and chose to use that assistance.
>
> I adopted the work in the form presented.
>
> I chose to put my name on it.
>
> I take responsibility for releasing it.

That is not a technical footnote.

It is a social contract.

And as the boundary between human and machine creation continues to dissolve, it may become one of the most important declarations a person can make.

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

# **You used the machine. You own the result.**
